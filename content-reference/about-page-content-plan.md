## Why this website exists

Sometimes you need software built. Sometimes you need someone to make sense of what you already have. These are a few situations where an engineer can help:

- You have a product idea and people willing to test it, but no developer. You need help defining and building a first version within your budget.
- You've paid for an application, but key features still fail. You need someone to review what exists and work out what can be fixed.
- Your business depends on software maintained by one person, and you can't justify a full-time hire. You need another engineer to get familiar with it and help with agreed work.
- Your team wants automated reports or answers drawn from company documents. You need those AI tools connected to your data and reliable enough for everyday work.
- You miss customer calls while working or meeting clients, and follow-ups slip. You want to explore an AI voice agent that captures enquiries and knows when to hand the call to a person.

I made this site so you can match that need with my services, check the work behind them, and ask whether I can help.

## What that means for your project

Choosing an engineer also means choosing someone to help you navigate costs, unfamiliar terms, and decisions that affect your product. Here are three ways I put my experience to work for you.

### Lower costs

I'll compare technologies and tools by what they cost to build with, maintain, and use. That includes development effort, hosting, storage, and AI or API usage. I'll look for options that meet your needs without paying for unnecessary complexity, and explain the tradeoffs. The aim is to keep costs manageable for you and the people using your product.

### Technical terms explained

You shouldn't have to learn a developer's vocabulary just to make a decision about your own product. When I mention an API, hosting, or an AI model, I'll explain what it does in your project and what it means for the people using it. You can ask questions, understand the choices, and tell me when something doesn't fit your needs.

### Decisions explained

A recommendation should come with a reason. I'll explain why a particular approach fits your project, what the alternatives would change, and where the compromises are. We'll consider your timeline, budget, existing software, and future plans. That gives you a basis for agreeing to the work and understanding what a change in direction would involve.
